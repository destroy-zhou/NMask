Beyond the target (i.e., endogenous) series itself, many real-world forecasting scenarios involve exogenous variables, which provide complementary information that cannot be inferred solely from historical endogenous observations. Depending on their availability, exogenous variables can be categorized into historical exogenous variables and future exogenous variables. The latter, which can be obtained using mature techniques, are particularly valuable because they provide direct information about the forecast horizon. For example, future weather forecasts can improve traffic prediction, while holiday schedules and promotional campaigns can enhance retail demand forecasting.

Consequently, forecasting with known future covariates has attracted increasing attention. Existing approaches \citep{ASTNet_2025} typically attempt to incorporate future exogenous variables through sophisticated feature interaction mechanisms. For example, recent methods such as TimeXer \citep{Timxer_2024} and TiDE \citep{tide_2023} explicitly model temporal or causal relationships between endogenous and exogenous variables via neural networks. Although these approaches achieve promising results, they are built upon a common assumption: future exogenous variables should be treated as additional features that need to be fused into the historical sequence representation.

We argue that this perspective overlooks the most important characteristic of future exogenous variables. Unlike ordinary auxiliary features \citep{InjectTST_2025, OLIVARES2023884}, future covariates are naturally associated with known future timestamps. In other words, they not only provide semantic information but also implicitly define the temporal structure of the prediction horizon. Existing methods \citep{pmlr-v267-hu25ac, GinAR_2025, 10105527} focus primarily on learning increasingly complex interactions between endogenous and exogenous variables, while largely neglecting this inherent temporal prior. As a result, they often require heavy architectures, introduce unnecessary modeling complexity, and may even amplify noise through excessive feature interactions.

First, \emph{how can future target representations effectively aggregate information from known future covariates?} Future covariates carry both variable-specific information and an explicit correspondence to future intervals. For example, a planned intervention may affect only a particular portion of the forecast horizon. Exploiting this information requires representations that connect the target segments to be predicted with the relevant future covariate observations. This motivates examining how the prediction horizon is represented, alongside how historical and exogenous information interact.

% [修改 P03：问题二——部分未知时，如何表示未知协变量未来并引导其学习]
Second, \emph{how can forecasting benefit from covariates whose future values are unavailable?} Complete future information is not always accessible: calendar features may be known while future sensor measurements remain unknown. Historical observations of these covariates can still contain useful information about their evolution. However, their future trajectories must be inferred rather than supplied as inputs. A forecasting model therefore needs to accommodate observed and unobserved covariate futures together and provide a learning signal for the latter, while respecting the information available at prediction time.

% [修改 P04：两个问题提出后，再介绍 NMask 及全部已知时的解决方法]
To address these questions, we propose NMask, a forecasting framework based on \emph{Masked Alignment}. For known future covariates, NMask introduces independently learnable future tokens to represent unknown target patches and aligns them with observed future covariate patches on a common temporal grid. Each token is associated with a target variable and future patch position. Its parameters are shared across samples, while its contextualized representation depends on the input. Through temporal and cross-variable attention, these tokens aggregate information from historical observations and available future covariates to form representations for predicting the corresponding target segments. Furthermore, NMask adopts a lightweight prediction paradigm. Instead of using a standard predictor \citep{huang2026storm, NEURIPS2025_571c7e16, NEURIPS2025_54c9bfb0} that maps all historical representations to the entire forecasting horizon, each learnable token is responsible for predicting a single future endogenous patch through a shared predictor. This design reduces the predictor complexity from $O(MdSP)$ to $O(dP)$, where $M$ and $S$ denote the numbers of historical and future patches, $d$ is the hidden dimension, and $P$ is the patch length. As a result, NMask avoids the parameter explosion caused by horizon-wise prediction and achieves substantially higher parameter efficiency while maintaining strong forecasting performance.

% [修改 P05：最后介绍部分未知时的扩展——协变量未来 token 与辅助预测监督]
For partially available future covariates, NMask retains the observed future patches and introduces learnable future tokens for the unavailable covariates. These tokens interact with target tokens and observed patches within the same encoder. We further introduce an auxiliary covariate prediction objective: future covariate observations from completed training windows supervise the corresponding tokens while remaining excluded from the forecasting input. This objective explicitly constrains the covariate future representations alongside target forecasting. Target and covariate forecasts are decoded from their respective contextualized representations, with information exchange taking place before decoding. At inference, NMask uses historical observations and available future covariates without requiring the withheld values.
