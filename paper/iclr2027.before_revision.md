\begin{abstract}
Time series forecasting with exogenous variables is crucial in many real-world applications, as exogenous information often provides complementary signals beyond the target series itself. In particular, future exogenous variables, when available, offer informative priors about future dynamics and can substantially improve forecasting performance. However, existing methods often treat future exogenous variables as auxiliary features to be fused with historical observations, leading to heavy architectures and inefficient interaction modeling. In this paper, we revisit this problem from a new perspective and reformulate time series forecasting with exogenous variables as a masked prediction problem. Based on this insight, we propose NMask, a lightweight network incorporating a Masked Alignment mechanism that explicitly leverages the temporal priors embedded in future exogenous variables. In addition, NMask introduces a compact shared prediction paradigm, enabling efficient forecasting while avoiding the parameter overhead of conventional horizon-wise predictors. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmarks demonstrate that NMask consistently outperforms existing exogenous-aware forecasting methods, achieving state-of-the-art forecasting performance, with significantly fewer parameters and lower computational cost. Code is available at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/ATM}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Time series forecasting is a fundamental task with widespread applications in finance \citep{Bridge_2026, Hu2025a, Wang2024FreDFLT}, transportation \citep{wang2026quadratic, wu_2026, Qiu2025ACS, shao_2025, xu_2023}, energy systems \citep{NEURIPS2025_0cd62dea, liu2026astgi, guo_2015}, industrial manufacturing \citep{CHENG2026103798, 11329174, 10265128}, and health \citep{Wang2025DistDFTF, CHENG2026103798, Wu2025MillGNNLM, wei_2022}. Accurate forecasts enable proactive decision-making and resource allocation by estimating future values from historical observations. Recent advances \citep{liu2026SE-LLM, ma2026phat, 2026timemosaic, TimeCMA_2025} in deep learning have significantly improved forecasting performance by modeling complex temporal dependencies within multivariate time series.

Beyond the target (i.e., endogenous) series itself, many real-world forecasting scenarios involve exogenous variables, which provide complementary information that cannot be inferred solely from historical endogenous observations. Depending on their availability, exogenous variables can be categorized into historical exogenous variables and future exogenous variables. The latter, which can be obtained using mature techniques, are particularly valuable because they provide direct information about the forecast horizon. For example, future weather forecasts can improve traffic prediction, while holiday schedules and promotional campaigns can enhance retail demand forecasting.

Consequently, forecasting with known future covariates has attracted increasing attention. Existing approaches \citep{ASTNet_2025} typically attempt to incorporate future exogenous variables through sophisticated feature interaction mechanisms. For example, recent methods such as TimeXer \citep{Timxer_2024} and TiDE \citep{tide_2023} explicitly model temporal or causal relationships between endogenous and exogenous variables via neural networks. Although these approaches achieve promising results, they are built upon a common assumption: future exogenous variables should be treated as additional features that need to be fused into the historical sequence representation.

We argue that this perspective overlooks the most important characteristic of future exogenous variables. Unlike ordinary auxiliary features \citep{InjectTST_2025, OLIVARES2023884}, future covariates are naturally associated with known future timestamps. In other words, they not only provide semantic information but also implicitly define the temporal structure of the prediction horizon. Existing methods \citep{pmlr-v267-hu25ac, GinAR_2025, 10105527} focus primarily on learning increasingly complex interactions between endogenous and exogenous variables, while largely neglecting this inherent temporal prior. As a result, they often require heavy architectures, introduce unnecessary modeling complexity, and may even amplify noise through excessive feature interactions.

This observation motivates a different formulation of forecasting with exogenous variables. Instead of viewing future covariates as external information to be injected into historical representations, we view them as temporal anchors that specify where future predictions should be generated. Under this perspective, forecasting can be reformulated as a masked prediction problem: the future endogenous sequence is regarded as a set of missing segments aligned with the known future exogenous timeline. The objective is therefore not to fuse exogenous information into historical features, but to recover the masked future endogenous variables conditioned on aligned future exogenous variables.

Based on this insight, we propose NMask, a lightweight forecasting framework based on Masked Alignment. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:method comparison}, NMask reformulates forecasting with future exogenous variables as a masked prediction task. Specifically, future exogenous patches are treated as temporal anchors, while a sequence of learnable tokens is appended after historical endogenous patches to represent the missing future target segments. By explicitly aligning these learnable tokens with future exogenous patches, NMask directly exploits the temporal prior embedded in known future covariates and predicts future endogenous values through masked reconstruction.

Furthermore, NMask adopts a lightweight prediction paradigm. Instead of using a standard predictor \citep{huang2026storm, NEURIPS2025_571c7e16, NEURIPS2025_54c9bfb0} that maps all historical representations to the entire forecasting horizon, each learnable token is responsible for predicting a single future endogenous patch through a shared predictor. This design reduces the predictor complexity from $O(MdSP)$ to $O(dP)$, where $M$ and $S$ denote the numbers of historical and future patches, $d$ is the hidden dimension, and $P$ is the patch length. As a result, NMask avoids the parameter explosion caused by horizon-wise prediction and achieves substantially higher parameter efficiency while maintaining strong forecasting performance.

% The proposed Masked Alignment mechanism offers two key advantages. First, it establishes a one-to-one correspondence between future exogenous patches and future prediction segments, allowing the model to directly exploit the temporal prior embedded in known future covariates. Second, by replacing complex graph reasoning and causal injection modules with learnable masked tokens, NMask achieves substantially lower computational complexity while preserving strong forecasting capability. In this sense, NMask redefines the role of future exogenous variables from features to be fused into anchors to be aligned.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1em] % 将缩进调整为 1em
\item We revisit forecasting with exogenous variables from a new perspective and reformulate it as a masked prediction problem, where future exogenous variables serve as temporal anchors rather than auxiliary features to be fused.

\item We propose NMask, a lightweight forecasting framework that aligns learnable masked tokens with future exogenous patches, enabling direct prediction of future endogenous series under explicit temporal prior.

\item We introduce a shared predictor paradigm, where each learnable token predicts a future endogenous patch through a shared prediction head. Compared with conventional horizon-wise predictors, this design reduces the predictor parameters from $O(MdSP)$ to $O(dP)$, achieving substantial parameter efficiency while maintaining strong forecasting performance.

\item We conduct extensive experiments on 12 real-world datasets with exogenous variables. The
results show that NMask achieves state-of-the-art forecasting performance while maintaining substantially lower model complexity and computational cost.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Time Series Forecasting with Patch}

% In recent years, patch-based methods \citep{PatchTST_2023a,luo2023_Cross-LKTCN} have received increasing attention in time series forecasting. Compared to traditional approaches \citep{zhang2023Crossformer,SparseTSF} that do not utilize patches, these models demonstrate significant performance improvements, highlighting the importance of enhancing local information through patching. For instance, PatchTST \citep{PatchTST_2023a} divides each time series into multiple patches and employs a self-attention mechanism to model dependencies across the temporal dimension, achieving state-of-the-art predictive performance. Following the success of PatchTST, recent studies such as PatchMLP \citep{PatchMLP_2025} and AMD \citep{AMD_2024} have also adopted patch-based strategies to enrich sequence representations and further improve downstream task performance.

% However, all these methods overlook the challenges associated with fixed patch lengths, which can lead to difficulties in modeling heterogeneous temporal patterns and to the truncation of temporal structures. Although methods like HDMixer \citep{HDMixer_2024} use extendable patches, their fixed number of patches prevents full coverage of the time series, leaving some time steps unrepresented. In this paper, we introduce the Temporal Tokenization Module to overcome the limitations of fixed-length patching strategies. This module can adaptively adjust both the length and the number of patches, thereby enhancing the usability of local semantic information within patches.

Recent advances \citep{liu2026apn, Times2D_2025, wang2025timemixer++, luo2023_Cross-LKTCN} in time series forecasting have been significantly influenced by patch-based representation learning, which draws inspiration from vision transformers. Instead of modeling time series at the individual timestamp level, patch-based methods \citep{CALF_2025, GPT4TS} divide sequences into contiguous segments (patches), thereby reducing sequence length and enhancing local information.

PatchTST \citep{PatchTST_2023a} is a representative work in this direction, showing that patching improves long-term forecasting by enabling more stable temporal representations and reducing token-level redundancy. Operating on patch-level tokens simplifies optimization and strengthens the ability of Transformers to capture long-term dependencies. Building on this idea, several works \citep{HDMixer_2024} explore further refinements of patch-based modeling. iTransformer \citep{liu2024itransformer} inverts the attention mechanism to model dependencies across variables rather than time steps, offering an alternative perspective on tokenization for multivariate forecasting. Recent studies such as PatchMLP \citep{PatchMLP_2025} and AMD \citep{AMD_2024} have also adopted patch-based strategies to enrich sequence representations and further improve downstream task performance.

Despite their success, most patch-based methods primarily focus on improving the encoding of historical sequences, while the decoding stage remains relatively simple, typically relying on direct projection or horizon-wise forecasting heads. This design overlooks the opportunity to explicitly structure the prediction process according to the temporal semantics of the forecast horizon. In contrast, NMask extends patch-based modeling by introducing masked alignment between learnable tokens and future temporal anchors, enabling a structured and lightweight decoding paradigm.

\subsection{Time Series Forecasting with Exogenous Variables}

Time series forecasting with exogenous variables has been extensively studied in both classical statistical and deep learning fields. Early models such as ARIMAX \citep{arimax_2001} and SARIMAX \citep{SARIMAX_2016} incorporate external covariates into autoregressive frameworks, showing that exogenous information can improve forecasting accuracy when properly aligned with target series.

With the rise of deep learning, various architectures have been proposed to better exploit exogenous signals. Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) \citep{TFT_2021} integrates historical and known future covariates via attention and gating mechanisms for flexible feature selection. TiDE \citep{tide_2023} uses an MLP-based design that concatenates covariates with endogenous inputs. TimeXer \citep{Timxer_2024} introduces patch-wise embeddings to relax strict temporal alignment, while CrossLinear \citep{CrossLinear_2025} models endogenous–exogenous interactions via cross-correlation embeddings. ExoTST \citep{ExoTST_2024} leverages cross-attention for handling time lags and missing data. Chronos-2 \citep{Chronos2_2025} exploits future exogenous variables through group-wise information sharing, but does not explicitly model them as temporal anchors. DAG \citep{DAG_2026} enhances forecasting by learning and injecting temporal–channel correlation structures to better exploit exogenous variables. GCGNet \citep{GCGNet_2026} further models variable dependencies using graph structures, but does not explicitly distinguish past and future covariates.

Although these methods differ in architecture, they generally incorporate future exogenous variables through feature interaction or information aggregation. This results in increasingly complex interaction designs, while overlooking that future covariates inherently define the temporal structure of the forecasting horizon. Consequently, they introduce additional complexity without fully exploiting alignment between future covariates and target future positions. In contrast, NMask formulates forecasting with exogenous variables as a masked prediction problem, where future covariates act as temporal anchors for generating future endogenous patches.

\section{Preliminaries}

\subsection{Definitions}

\textbf{Definition 3.1 (Time Series).}
Let $\rmZ \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T}$ denote a multivariate time series, where $T$ represents the number of temporal observations and $N$ denotes the number of observed variables. Each entry $Z_{i,t}$ corresponds to the value of the $i$-th variable at time step $t$. When $N=1$, the series reduces to a univariate case; otherwise, it is multivariate.

\textbf{Definition 3.2 (Endogenous Series).}
We define the endogenous time series $\rmX^{\text{endo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T}$ as the set of target variables whose future values need to be predicted. These variables evolve according to latent system dynamics and may be influenced by both their historical states and external signals.

\textbf{Definition 3.3 (Exogenous Series).}
Exogenous variables refer to auxiliary covariates that are not prediction targets but provide complementary information for forecasting. We assume they are often known or can be estimated in advance. Formally, we split them into:
\[
\rmX^{\text{exo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times T}, \quad \rmY^{\text{exo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times F},
\]
where $D$ is the number of exogenous variates, $T$ is the historical length, and $F$ is the forecasting horizon. The future exogenous sequence $\rmY^{\text{exo}}$ is assumed to be available.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

\textbf{Exogenous-Aware Forecasting.}
Given historical endogenous observations $\rmX^{\text{endo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T}$, historical exogenous variables $\rmX^{\text{exo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times T}$, and future exogenous variables $\rmY^{\text{exo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times F}$, the objective is to estimate future endogenous values over a horizon of length $F$. We aim to learn a parameterized mapping $F_{\theta}$ such that:
\[
\hat{\rmY}^{\text{endo}} = F_{\theta}(\rmX^{\text{endo}}, \rmX^{\text{exo}}, \rmY^{\text{exo}}),
\]
where $\hat{\rmY}^{\text{endo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times F}$ denotes the predicted future endogenous series.
