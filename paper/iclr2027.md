\begin{abstract}
Future covariates provide information about the forecast horizon and can improve time series forecasting. We first study how to use this information when future covariates are fully available. We propose NMask, a forecasting framework that represents unknown future target patches with learnable queries and aligns them with observed future covariate patches on a common temporal grid. A shared encoder updates these representations through temporal and cross-variable interactions, while a shared patch prediction head maps each target query to its corresponding future segment. We then extend this formulation to settings where only a subset of covariates is available into the future. NMask introduces learnable queries for the unavailable future covariates and uses their future observations from training windows as auxiliary prediction labels. This objective explicitly constrains the covariate future representations while keeping unavailable values outside the forecasting input. The resulting formulation connects the use of observed future covariates with the joint prediction of unobserved future variables.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Time series forecasting is a fundamental task with widespread applications in finance \citep{Bridge_2026, Hu2025a, Wang2024FreDFLT}, transportation \citep{wang2026quadratic, wu_2026, Qiu2025ACS, shao_2025, xu_2023}, energy systems \citep{NEURIPS2025_0cd62dea, liu2026astgi, guo_2015}, industrial manufacturing \citep{CHENG2026103798, 11329174, 10265128}, and health \citep{Wang2025DistDFTF, CHENG2026103798, Wu2025MillGNNLM, wei_2022}. Accurate forecasts enable proactive decision-making and resource allocation by estimating future values from historical observations. Recent advances \citep{liu2026SE-LLM, ma2026phat, 2026timemosaic, TimeCMA_2025} in deep learning have significantly improved forecasting performance by modeling complex temporal dependencies within multivariate time series.

% [修改 M02：以预测时刻的可用信息定义协变量，避免把天气预报等同于真实未来观测]
Beyond the target series, many forecasting tasks include covariates that provide complementary information. Their usefulness depends in part on what is available at the forecast origin. Calendar features and planned interventions may be known throughout the prediction horizon, whereas other covariates may only have historical observations. Forecasts of external quantities may also be available, but these must be distinguished from subsequently realized values. We begin with the setting in which the future values supplied for all covariates are available at prediction time, and then consider partial availability.

% [修改 M03：保留已知未来协变量为第一研究问题，删去“现有方法普遍忽视时间对齐”的概括]
Forecasting with future covariates has motivated a range of neural architectures \citep{TFT_2021, tide_2023, ASTNet_2025, DAG_2026}. These methods differ in how they encode covariates and combine them with target representations. Our starting question concerns the representation of the prediction horizon itself: \emph{how can each future target segment directly interact with the covariate information available for the same interval?} This question motivates a formulation in which observed future covariates and the target segments to be predicted occupy corresponding positions in a common patch grid.

% [修改 M04：将 Masked Alignment 具体化为独立可学习目标查询、同位置交互，避免宣称新注意力或因果机制]
We propose NMask, which introduces independently learnable queries for future target patches. Each query is associated with a target variable and a future patch position. Historical target patches, historical covariate patches, observed future covariate patches, and future target queries are processed by a shared encoder. Temporal attention connects patches within each variable, and cross-variable attention exchanges information at corresponding patch positions. We refer to this arrangement as \emph{Masked Alignment}: unknown future target segments are represented by queries that are aligned with the available covariate observations. Here, masking refers to withholding unknown values from the input, rather than imposing a causal attention constraint.

% [修改 M05：保留共享预测头，明确 O(MdSP) -> O(dP) 是特定预测头的参数量比较]
Each updated target query predicts one future patch through a shared prediction head. Relative to a dense linear head that maps all $M$ historical patch embeddings to $S$ future patches, this reduces the leading-order prediction-head parameter count from $O(MdSP)$ to $O(dP)$, where $d$ is the embedding dimension and $P$ is the patch length. This comparison concerns the prediction head. The encoder and learnable queries remain part of the total model, and a smaller head does not by itself establish lower overall computation or latency. The shared head provides a compact readout for the query-based representation.

% [新增 M06：第二研究问题——从全部已知自然过渡到部分协变量未来不可用]
The fully known setting, however, does not cover tasks in which only some covariates are available over the forecast horizon. For example, calendar features can remain available while future sensor measurements are unknown. In this case, some observed future covariate patches can no longer be supplied to the encoder. This raises a second question: \emph{how should the model represent these unknown covariate futures, and what supervision should guide their representations?}

% [新增 M07：部分未知时的改进——协变量未来查询 + 显式预测监督；明确标签不作为输入]
We extend NMask by introducing learnable future queries for the unavailable covariates, while retaining observed future patches for the available ones. The same encoder jointly updates the target and covariate representations using the permitted historical and future inputs. To explicitly constrain the unknown covariate representations, we add an auxiliary prediction objective using their future observations from completed training windows. These observations serve only as labels for variables designated unavailable at the forecast origin. The model predicts target and covariate futures from shared hidden representations; it does not require a separate stage that feeds decoded covariate forecasts back into the target predictor.

% [新增 M08：限定与 Chronos-2 的区别，不再把时间/变量注意力或部分可用本身写成独有能力]
Our contribution concerns this query parameterization and its supervision within covariate forecasting. Patch-based future representations and temporal/cross-variable attention are shared with existing approaches, including Chronos-2 \citep{Chronos2_2025}; support for partially available covariates alone is not the distinction. We first examine learnable target queries under fully known future covariates, and then investigate how explicit covariate future queries and auxiliary supervision extend this formulation to partial availability.

% [修改 M09：贡献按“全部已知的结构与预测头 -> 部分未知的扩展 -> 实验”排列]
Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1em]
\item We propose Masked Alignment for forecasting with known future covariates. Independently learnable target queries are aligned with observed future covariate patches and updated through temporal and cross-variable interactions to construct representations for future target segments.
\item We use a shared patch prediction head to decode the future target queries. Compared with a dense flattened-history-to-horizon head, the leading-order head parameter count decreases from $O(MdSP)$ to $O(dP)$.
\item We extend the formulation to partially available future covariates by representing unavailable covariate futures with learnable queries and introducing an explicit covariate prediction auxiliary objective. This jointly constrains future representations without requiring unavailable covariate values as inference inputs.
% [待核对] 第三项需要同一样本内混合已知/未知协变量的实现与实验支撑。
\item We evaluate forecasting on 12 real-world datasets with covariates and analyze the effects of future-query learnability and covariate prediction supervision.

\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

% [修改 M10：压缩 patch 相关工作，去掉共享解码和未来 patch 属于独有创新的暗示]
\subsection{Patch-Based Time Series Forecasting}

Patch-based methods represent contiguous observations as tokens, allowing sequence models to operate at a coarser temporal resolution \citep{PatchTST_2023a, zhang2023Crossformer, CALF_2025, GPT4TS}. Subsequent work explores different patch representations and architectures \citep{HDMixer_2024, PatchMLP_2025, AMD_2024, liu2026apn, Times2D_2025, wang2025timemixer++, luo2023_Cross-LKTCN}. NMask uses patching as a representation choice and focuses on how future segments are parameterized and supervised. Its future queries are directly optimized parameters associated with variables and forecast positions, and their updated representations are decoded through a shared patch head. Patching and shared decoding are not claimed as new concepts in isolation.

% [修改 M11：保留外生预测相关工作，删除未经支持的复杂度、因果关系及忽视时间结构的概括]
\subsection{Forecasting with Exogenous Variables}

Exogenous-aware forecasting incorporates additional covariates alongside target histories. Classical examples include ARIMAX \citep{arimax_2001} and SARIMAX \citep{SARIMAX_2016}. Neural approaches include TFT \citep{TFT_2021}, TiDE \citep{tide_2023}, TimeXer \citep{Timxer_2024}, CrossLinear \citep{CrossLinear_2025}, ExoTST \citep{ExoTST_2024}, DAG \citep{DAG_2026}, and GCGNet \citep{GCGNet_2026}. These works motivate studying both covariate integration and the information available at the forecast origin. NMask first studies direct interaction between target future queries and observed future covariate patches, then extends the same representation to covariates whose future observations are unavailable.

% [新增 M12：单列 Chronos-2，替换原稿“does not explicitly model them as temporal anchors”的不充分对比]
\subsection{Relation to Chronos-2}

Chronos-2 \citep{Chronos2_2025} supports univariate, multivariate, and covariate-informed forecasting through pretraining and group attention. It alternates temporal attention with information sharing across series at corresponding patch positions. Unknown future values are represented using missing-value indicators and time features passed through a trainable patch embedding network. NMask instead uses independently parameterized future queries for unknown target and covariate patches. This is a distinction in parameterization; it does not mean that Chronos-2's future embeddings are untrainable.

For covariate-defined tasks, the public Chronos-2 training dataset masks covariate future labels out of the forecasting loss. NMask adds explicit prediction supervision for covariates whose future values are withheld from the input. This comparison concerns the auxiliary training objective in a covariate-defined task, not an absence of multivariate prediction supervision in Chronos-2's pretraining.

\section{Preliminaries}

% [修改 M13：统一 N/D/T/F 的含义，区分主预测目标与协变量辅助标签]
\subsection{Notation}

Let $\rmX^{\text{endo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T}$ and $\rmX^{\text{exo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times T}$ denote historical observations of $N$ target variables and $D$ covariates, respectively. Their future sequences over a horizon of length $F$ are $\rmY^{\text{endo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times F}$ and $\rmY^{\text{exo}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times F}$. The target future is always excluded from the forecasting input. A covariate is not a primary prediction target, although its future may be used as an auxiliary training label.

% [修改 M14：先定义原稿的全部已知任务，保留其核心映射]
\subsection{Forecasting with Known Future Covariates}

We first assume that the future sequences of all $D$ covariates are available at the forecast origin. The task is to learn
\[
\hat{\rmY}^{\text{endo}}
= f_{\theta}\!\left(\rmX^{\text{endo}}, \rmX^{\text{exo}}, \rmY^{\text{exo}}\right).
\]
Available inputs must reflect information accessible when the forecast is issued. If a covariate is itself a forecast, its issued forecast values must not be replaced by subsequently realized observations.

% [新增 M15：定义部分未知的扩展；限定变量级可用性，避免声称任意时间点缺失已实现]
\subsection{Extension to Partially Available Future Covariates}

We partition the covariate indices into known and unknown sets $\mathcal{K}$ and $\mathcal{U}$, with $\mathcal{K} \cup \mathcal{U}=\{1,\ldots,D\}$ and $\mathcal{K} \cap \mathcal{U}=\varnothing$. Covariates in $\mathcal{K}$ have available values throughout the forecast horizon; those in $\mathcal{U}$ have historical observations only. The forecasting task becomes
\[
\hat{\rmY}^{\text{endo}}
= f_{\theta}\!\left(
\rmX^{\text{endo}}, \rmX^{\text{exo}},
\rmY^{\text{exo}}_{\mathcal{K}}, \mathcal{K}
\right).
\]
This definition covers the fully known case ($\mathcal{U}=\varnothing$) and the fully unknown case ($\mathcal{K}=\varnothing$). We focus on variable-level availability; arbitrary missing time points within an otherwise known future covariate are outside this formulation.

During training, realized future observations for variables in $\mathcal{U}$ may be available in completed historical windows. They can be used as auxiliary labels while remaining excluded from the input. We denote by $\Omega_{\mathcal{U}}$ the set of unknown-covariate future entries for which such labels are observed.

\section{NMask}

\subsection{Masked Alignment with Known Future Covariates}

We partition historical and future intervals into patches of length $P$, with $M$ historical patches and $S$ future patches after padding. Let $e_{\phi}$ denote the patch embedding map. For target variable $n$ and future patch $s$, we introduce a learnable query
\[
\mathbf{q}^{\text{endo}}_{n,s} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}.
\]
These parameters are shared across samples, and their contextualized representations depend on the observations of each sample. Historical target and covariate patches are embedded with $e_{\phi}$, as are the known future covariate patches. Queries and observed future embeddings occupy matching positions in the temporal patch grid.

A shared encoder alternates temporal attention within variables and cross-variable attention at corresponding patch positions. Thus, a target query can incorporate historical dynamics and future covariate information through the encoder. Given its final representation $\mathbf{h}^{\text{endo}}_{n,s}$, a shared head produces
\[
\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{\text{endo}}_{n,s}
= g_{\psi}\!\left(\mathbf{h}^{\text{endo}}_{n,s}\right),
\qquad
g_{\psi}: \mathbb{R}^{d} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{P}.
\]
Predicted patches are concatenated and trimmed to the requested horizon.

For a linear $g_{\psi}$, the head contains $dP+P$ parameters. A dense head from a flattened $Md$-dimensional historical representation to $SP$ future values contains $MdSP+SP$ parameters. The target queries themselves add $NSd$ parameters, so the head-level reduction does not imply that the full model parameter count is independent of the horizon.
% [待核对] 上式为预测区间按 P 补齐到 SP 的比较；若基线直接输出 F，则对应精确参数量为 MdF+F。

% [新增 M17：部分可用时，已知协变量嵌入观测，未知协变量使用可学习查询]
\subsection{Future Queries for Unavailable Covariates}

For partially available covariates, the future representation of covariate $j$ at patch $s$ is constructed as
\[
\mathbf{z}^{\text{exo}}_{j,s}
=
\begin{cases}
e_{\phi}\!\left(\mathbf{y}^{\text{exo}}_{j,s}\right), & j \in \mathcal{K},\\
\mathbf{q}^{\text{exo}}_{j,s}, & j \in \mathcal{U},
\end{cases}
\]
where $\mathbf{q}^{\text{exo}}_{j,s} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is a learnable covariate future query. The unknown branch does not read the withheld future observation. Preprocessing statistics must likewise exclude unavailable future values.

The encoder jointly updates the target queries, unknown-covariate queries, and observed patches. An auxiliary head $g_{\eta}$ maps contextualized covariate queries to future covariate predictions. Target and covariate outputs are decoded from their respective hidden representations, so the interaction takes place before decoding. In the fully known case, no unknown-covariate queries or auxiliary predictions are required.

% [新增 M18：辅助监督只作用于不可作为输入、但训练时有真实标签的未来协变量]
\subsection{Covariate Prediction Supervision}

Let $\ell$ be the point prediction loss. We optimize
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{target}}
=
\frac{1}{NF}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{t=1}^{F}
\ell\!\left(\hat{Y}^{\text{endo}}_{n,t},Y^{\text{endo}}_{n,t}\right)
\]
together with
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{1}{|\Omega_{\mathcal{U}}|}
\sum_{(j,t)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{U}}}
\ell\!\left(\hat{Y}^{\text{exo}}_{j,t},Y^{\text{exo}}_{j,t}\right),
& |\Omega_{\mathcal{U}}|>0,\\
0, & |\Omega_{\mathcal{U}}|=0.
\end{cases}
\]
The total objective is
\[
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{target}}+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}},
\qquad \alpha \geq 0.
\]
The auxiliary loss directly supervises the unknown covariate futures and updates the shared encoder. Its effect on target forecasting is assessed through ablations. At inference, the model uses historical observations, available future covariates, and learned query parameters; the auxiliary labels are not needed.
